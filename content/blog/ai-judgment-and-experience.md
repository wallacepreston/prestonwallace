What happens to experienced professionals as AI gets better at analysis?

I recently discussed that question with [Richard Munro](https://www.linkedin.com/in/richardlmunro/), an independent board director, financial expert, and court-appointed fiduciary with more than 35 years of executive and board experience.

His answer was practical: AI will absorb more mechanical work. The harder work still depends on experience, judgment, and accountability.

## AI raises the value of judgment

Richard expects AI to handle more repetitive accounting, testing, document review, and information processing. He also believes a young accountant should spend serious time learning LLMs and finding ways to work more effectively with them.

AI can analyze a large body of information faster than a team of people. The final recommendation still sits inside a larger situation involving incomplete facts, competing interests, legal duties, and real consequences.

That is where professional judgment matters. Judgment grows through repeated exposure to difficult decisions and feedback from their outcomes. A model can support that process with stronger analysis. The accountable professional must decide what the evidence means and what should happen next.

## Client selection is part of the craft

Early in a consulting career, it is tempting to accept every available project. Over time, choosing the right clients becomes part of doing good work.

Richard described difficult clients as the noisiest and most demanding. They can consume attention that should go toward solving the actual problem. His willingness to tell a client, “You can fire me. It’s OK,” reflects the independence required in fiduciary and advisory roles.

Clear boundaries protect the quality of the work. They also make honest advice possible.

## A useful pattern for AI engineering

Richard’s work suggests a simple design principle for professional AI systems:

Use AI to collect evidence, identify anomalies, summarize records, test scenarios, and maintain an audit trail. Then place consequential decisions with a qualified person who understands the context and owns the outcome.

The goal is better judgment supported by better information. That is a valuable role for AI in accounting, corporate governance, restructuring, and other high-stakes fields.
