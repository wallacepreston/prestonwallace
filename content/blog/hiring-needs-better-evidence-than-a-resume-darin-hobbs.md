Resumes are a proxy for what a person can do. That proxy breaks down when employers receive hundreds of similar applications and candidates cannot show the skills behind a title.

I spoke with [Darin Hobbs](https://www.linkedin.com/in/darinhobbs/) about Learning and Employment Records, digital wallets, and the role AI can play in skills-based hiring.

## Match skills on both sides

Better records alone do not solve matching. Employers need job descriptions enriched with the skills and credentials they actually need. Then a system can compare evidence from a learner or worker with evidence from the role.

## A wallet should belong to the individual

A digital credential wallet should retrieve credentials from issuing systems and let its owner decide how to share them. This makes a wallet different from a social profile, where a person can publish a credential but may not control its source, visibility, or lifespan.

My takeaway: skills-based hiring becomes more useful when employers and candidates contribute structured, verifiable evidence to the same ecosystem.
